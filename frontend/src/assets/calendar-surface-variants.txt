CALENDAR SURFACE — TWO COLOUR VARIANTS
======================================
Saved 2026-08-17. Records both tints tried for the day tiles on /appointments
(version 2), so either can be put back without re-deriving the numbers.

THE PROBLEM THEY SOLVE
----------------------
The tiles started as #F6F8FA — the product's page ground — on a white card.
That is roughly 2% away from white: the edges of forty-two tiles had to be
hunted for, which is what made the grid tiring to look at. Both variants below
are the same fix (raise the contrast); they differ only in hue.

The opacities are matched, not copied: 15% of #999999 and 6% of #3248F2 land at
the same distance from white. So switching between them changes the cast and
nothing else.

Both stay under the 10–15% band the product reserves for real data, so the grid
reads as a surface rather than as something being pointed at.


VARIANT A — accent tint (cool)
------------------------------
base    bg-[#3248F2]/[0.06]     ≈ rgb(240,241,253)
hover   bg-[#3248F2]/[0.13]
outside bg-[#3248F2]/[0.03]     (days from the neighbouring month)
outside hover  bg-[#3248F2]/[0.08]
card    bg-[#3248F2]/[0.06]  hover:bg-[#3248F2]/[0.11]   (DayPanel booking)

Adds no new token — the tint falls out of the brand accent. Reads clearly
against white because a cool cast separates from white better than a neutral
does at the same lightness.


VARIANT B — muted grey (neutral)   [ACTIVE]
-------------------------------------------
base    bg-[#999999]/15         ≈ rgb(240,240,240)
hover   bg-[#999999]/28
outside bg-[#999999]/8
outside hover  bg-[#999999]/16
card    bg-[#999999]/15  hover:bg-[#999999]/25          (DayPanel booking)

Uses the palette's existing muted token. Neutral, so it competes with nothing;
the trade is that a grey at this lightness reads slightly flatter than the tint.

A THIRD DIRECTION, NOT BUILT
----------------------------
Drop the tile fill entirely: white cells separated by a 1px #999999/15 divider,
hover only. This is what CLAUDE.md's own rule prescribes ("never nest a card
inside a card; internal grouping is done with a 1px divider"), and it removes
the wash rather than tuning it. Not built because the reference design uses
tiles.
